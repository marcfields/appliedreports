## The company

The Applied Report Company is an engineering house. It builds the instruments that the next generation of building engineers will use to do their work, and it stands behind the documents that work becomes. Property condition assessments. Reserve fund studies. Roofing condition assessments. Site observation reports. The documents that govern how buildings are bought, sold, insured, repaired, and held.

These documents are the engineer's craft. They carry a weight. They are entered into a firm's archive. They are read decades after they are written. They are not deliverables. They are records.

The work of producing them got slow. Sorting hundreds of field photos. Transcribing notes from a clipboard. Pulling forward language from a report written eight years ago. None of that is the engineering. The engineering happens on the roof, in the boiler room, on the parking deck. The page is where the engineering gets put down. The page is where the company shows up.

The company is named *The Applied Report Company* because that is what the work is: the report, applied. Methodology brought to bear on a specific building, on a specific date, under a specific signature. The product, Arc, is the instrument the engineer reaches for to make this happen faster, cleaner, and with more pride than before.

The frame to hold: this is an engineering house, not a software company. It belongs to a category that includes AMCA, ARUP, Buro Happold, Knippers Helbig. Firms that are proud of what they make, proud of the people they make it for, and confident enough about both to let the work do the talking.

## The feeling

The brand should feel **alive, proud, and rooted**. That is the whole instruction. Read in order:

**Alive.** The engineering report has been treated as drudgery for a generation. The brand pushes back on that. The site, the materials, the product, the way the company shows up on a Tuesday morning. All of it carries heat. A senior engineer should look at the brand and feel that the work they have been doing for thirty years has been *seen*. Not romanticized. Seen. The dignity of the field photo. The dignity of the marked-up plan. The dignity of the third revision of a condition statement that finally reads right.

**Proud.** Pride in the engineer. Pride in the firms. Pride in the buildings. AMCA lists Boeing, Airbus, Lockheed Martin without flinching, and draws every aircraft platform it ships components for, with the year it entered service. The Applied Report Company holds itself the same way: glorifies the built world, reveres the systems the engineers assess. Great pride and care is taken in the work.

**Rooted.** In the trade. In the details. In the way things are done. In the physical building. In the built world. The brand never floats above the work. It sits in the work. The vocabulary is the engineer's vocabulary. The visual references are the engineer's visual references: structural drawings, schedules, plates, schematic colour, photographic plates of the built environment. Not "tech" references with engineering wallpaper.

What the brand should NOT feel like:
- A SaaS landing page. The category Arc is leaving.
- A research lab. An earlier direction reached too far into developer-tools territory.
- A reverent archive. An earlier draft overcorrected into a quiet, library-voice register. That is half of the answer. The other half is heat.
- A consumer AI product. Not warm, friendly, customer-success. The engineer is a peer.

The right combined register: the cover of an AMCA annual report, designed by someone who reads US Graphics for someone who inspects buildings. Confident, but not arrogant. Visually loud where the work earns it. Specific everywhere.

## Naming

**The Applied Report Company.** The legal name and the masthead. Used in footers, signatures, on the colophon of every report Arc produces. The cadence is institutional by construction: a company name in the lineage of *The Atchison, Topeka, and Santa Fe Railway*, *Pan American World Airways*, *Advanced Manufacturing Company of America*. The article matters. *The*. It is a company that means to be the company.

**Applied Reports.** The working name. The URL. Day-to-day surfaces. Reads cleanly.

**Arc.** The product. The instrument the engineer addresses. *Arc pulled the photos for this section. Open Arc.* The web app and the Word add-in are two surfaces of the same instrument. Arc is never compounded. Not ArcWrite, not ArcEngine. The base name carries the product.

## The audience

The brand speaks to a licensed practising engineer, 35 to 60, whose name is on the document and whose firm's name is on the cover. This reader has read more reports than they have written. They know what a good one looks like. They know the difference between the plumbing systems of every building in the city. They are not impressed by promises and they are not flattered by warmth.

What this reader responds to:
- Specificity. The trade's vocabulary used correctly.
- Visible craft. Care in the document, care in the page.
- Pride in the work, theirs and the company's.
- An instrument that respects their judgment and profession. 

What this reader rejects:
- Marketing language.
- Anything that suggests the engineering is being done *for* them by software.
- A tone that suggests the brand is doing them a favour.

The engineer is the protagonist. The Applied Report Company is the house that makes the bench, the lens, and the press they use.

## The references

**AMCA (Advanced Manufacturing Company of America).** The closest single reference. A holding company of legacy aerospace suppliers (Electro-Mech, Cal-Draulics, Aerospace Control Products, Electrocube, Payne Magnetics), every brand listed with its founding year. Customers listed without apology: Boeing, Airbus, Lockheed Martin, Gulfstream, Embraer. Aircraft platforms drawn as line art with year of service. The tone is plain, proud, institutional. The lesson: a company can roll up legacy work, show it confidently, and let the names speak. The Applied Report Company should look at this site and steal the *posture*.

**U.S. Graphics Company.** Neil Panchal's small operation in Phoenix. Berkeley Mono. Limited publications. *Relentlessly pursuing accurate and truthful interpretation.* A one-person house that holds its discipline so well it reads as an institution. The lesson: technical-graphics craft can be both contemporary and rooted. Engineering-drawing aesthetics, well-set monospace, schedules and catalogs that feel current. The visual reference for the *texture* of how Applied Reports does detail.

**The cinematic stills** (the alpine collage with the white grid building, the ironworker on the steel beam, the Pittsburgh skyline at dusk, the Cleveland Arcade interior). The lesson: imagery should carry weight. The brand earns its emotional register from real photographs of real buildings, real engineers, real cities at real moments. Not stock. Not isometric illustration. Not abstract product art. The image is doing work.

**Western Chemicals.** Held as a reference for *bravery*. One image, one declarative sentence, an email address. The site is not afraid to commit. The Applied Report Company should be willing to do the same on its primary surface: one image of consequence, one declaration of what the company does, the door open for a real conversation.

These references should be loaded together. The error mode is loading any one of them alone. AMCA without US Graphics goes too corporate. US Graphics without AMCA goes too small-press. Browser Company without either goes too consumer. The combination is the brand.

## Brand tenets

The principles to hold while making anything, in priority order. Earlier overrides later when they conflict.

**1. Specificity over abstraction, always.**
Name the building type. Name the system. Name the firm. Name the city. Name the document type. The brand's confidence comes from how specific it is willing to be. Never "buildings"; always "post-war concrete residential mid-rises in the GTA." Never "documents"; always "the Reserve Fund Study, Class 1." Never "engineers"; always "the principal engineer who signs off on the report." This is the AMCA move and it is the strongest one in the kit.

**2. Celebrate the engineer.**
The engineer is the protagonist of every page. Their craft, their judgment, their reputation. The brand exists to elevate them. When a feature is described, it is described in terms of what the engineer is now free to do, not what the software does. When a customer is shown, the customer's work is shown: the building they assessed, the firm they signed under, the year the report was issued.

**3. The page can be loud where the work earns it.**
Restraint is a tool, not the brand. The page can be large-scale. The image can be cinematic. The wordmark can run a full bleed. Pride is shown by giving the work room. Hold tight where it matters (metadata, captions, the colophon). Let go where it matters (the cover image, the section opener, the platform list, the firm roster).

**4. The wordmark is the brand's thesis.**
The serif-and-grotesque pairing in the Applied Reports and Arc marks is the brand argument expressed as type. A traditional serif at display scale holds the lineage of the engineering report. A clean grotesque underneath does the contemporary work. Keep both. The tension is the point.

**5. Imagery is photographic, real, and chosen with care.**
Real buildings. Real engineers. Real cities. The cinematic still over the rendered mockup. The archival photograph over the stock photo. The site photo from an actual inspection over anything art-directed. The brand's emotional weight comes from imagery that could not have been faked.

**6. Show the house, name the products.**
Browser Company is the model. The Applied Report Company has a voice, a values page, a careers page, a way of showing up. Arc is the product, with its own surface and its own mark. Don't collapse the two. The company is the institution; Arc is the instrument.

**7. The instrument is invisible inside the document.**

The engineer's name is on the report. The firm's name is on the cover. The report is in the firm's template and brand. The Applied Report Company's mark, if it appears at all, is in the add-in.

## Voice

The voice of an engineering house, not a software company. Direct. Specific. Proud of the work. Willing to commit.

Cadence: declarative. Short sentences when the sentence is doing one thing. Longer sentences when they are carrying detail that earns the length. No throat-clearing. No "we believe."

Vocabulary: the trade's. *Uniformat-classified. OCR-corrected handwritten field notes. The reserve fund study, Class 1. The roofing condition assessment, single-ply.* Names systems. Names codes. Names firms. Trusts the reader.

Personality: a confident senior engineer who runs a good firm. Has seen a thousand buildings. Is proud of the work and proud of the team. Tells you what is true. Doesn't oversell. Has nothing to prove and therefore nothing to hide.

What is *not* in the voice:
- Marketing language. *Supercharge, unlock, leverage, 10x, powerful, smart, intelligent, magical.*
- Category vocabulary. *Platform, infrastructure (as marketing word), suite, workspace, solution.*
- Founder warmth on company surfaces. *Hi, we're, we believe, we think.* The company speaks in third register on its own pages.
- False modesty. The work is good. Show that by being specific about it.

Style samples (directional, not final):

> *The Applied Report Company. Intelligence for the built world.*

> *The records that govern how the built environment is bought, sold, insured, and held.*

> *Arc is the instrument. It does not write the report. It clears the path between the field and the page.*

> *Trusted by [Firm], [Firm], [Firm], and others across North America.*

## Surfaces

The brand shows up across a handful of surfaces. Each one has a different job. The combined system is the brand.

**The website.** The masthead is the company. Below it, one or two images of consequence, a declaration of what the company does, the product journey described with their own surfaces to click through to. A short roster of report types. A short list of firms (when there are firms to list). A contact line. A colophon. The page should feel closer to AMCA than to a typical software landing page: confident, specific, image-led, no testimonial carousels, no "trusted by" badge wall.

**Arc.** Lives on its own surface. Has its own mark, its own voice (the instrument's voice: quiet, precise, helpful where it can be helpful), its own UI. The instrument is the engineer's reading lamp. Inside the document, Arc recedes. The web app and the Word add-in are the same instrument with two windows.

## Colour palette

The palette system uses five named roles consistently across all surfaces. The roles are fixed; the values vary by surface context.

**Roles**

- **Field** — the dominant ground. Covers the majority of any surface.
- **Tone** — a lighter or darker step within the same chromatic key as the field. Used for schematic colour, section markers, tonal blocks.
- **Type** — the colour all body and display type is set in. Must be legible against the field at small sizes.
- **Paper** — the light value, used for reverse cards, footers, and document surfaces where the field would be too heavy.
- **Mark** — the accent. Used at stamp-scale only: the colophon, callouts, revision marks, accents, highlights. Never used as a fill or a background.
